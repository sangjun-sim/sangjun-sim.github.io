<!-- REVIEW: [high] (p.3, the line "(1 1 1) P_E (x,y,z)^T = (1/3)(x+y+z) = 0") Arithmetic error, and the logical direction is inverted. With the matrix written just above, P_E = I - (1/3)J where J is the all-ones matrix, so every row and column of P_E sums to zero: (1,1,1) P_E = (0,0,0) identically. The product is therefore 0 for ANY vector, not (1/3)(x+y+z), and the boxed conclusion "x+y+z=0" cannot be derived by setting an identity to zero. The correct statement runs the other way: for arbitrary r, the projected vector v = P_E r automatically satisfies v_1+v_2+v_3 = 0, i.e. the E subspace IS the plane ker(1,1,1) = the orthogonal complement of the A_1 direction (1,1,1)^T. Suggested repair: drop the "= (1/3)(x+y+z)" step and instead state (1,1,1) P_E = 0 => range(P_E) subset {v : sum_i v_i = 0}, which is exactly the constraint used to build e_a and e_b below. -->

<!-- REVIEW: [high] (p.2, "R(g) is the representation matrix of the class g") Misstatement of a definition. The projector is P_mu = (d_mu/|G|) * sum over all |G| group elements g of chi_mu(g)^* R(g). The calculation that follows is in fact correct (P_{A_1} is written as a sum of all six element matrices R(I), R(C_3), R(C_3^2), R(C_2z), R(C_2x), R(C_2y)), so only the wording needs fixing. Flagging as high because the class-vs-element conflation, if taken literally, gives a wrong projector: substituting N_g * R(one representative) for the sum over a class is valid for characters but invalid for matrices. -->

<!-- REVIEW: [medium] (p.2, the 3x3 matrices R(C_3), R(C_2z)) Important physical caveat that is currently implicit: these matrices act on the three-dimensional space spanned by the bond operators {J_x^{xx}, J_y^{yy}, J_z^{zz}}, i.e. on their COEFFICIENTS, not on spin components. Cross-check worth including: the spin vector (S^x,S^y,S^z) has chi = (3, 0, -1) and decomposes as A_2 (+) E, not A_1 (+) E, so a Zeeman term along [111] is A_2 and not A_1-symmetric under the naive permutation picture. -->
